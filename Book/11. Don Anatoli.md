### (11) Don Anatoli 9/17/2026

{Gifted to Anatoli} [I am moving to Colombia and giving away my collection of paintings, each with its own story.]

Being in Colombia, I missed the funeral of my dear friend Anatoli. At 65 years old, he had asked me to find him a wife and gave me an AI-generated photo of his ideal woman. I did find her in Colombia. It was the best I could do to repay his invaluable help in searching for my 2nd wife.

I met him at the pool several years ago. I was working as a lifeguard and was depressed after being divorced for the 1st time in my life. He asked me, “Why isn't the hot tub working?” I replied, “It is broken.” He cheerfully said that he knew a guy who could fix it and told me to walk him to management. Instead, I proposed that he rent a room in the house where I lived. He agreed.

If he could facilitate fixing the hot tub, then I thought he might be capable of fixing my life. And I kindly asked Anatoli to be my love life consultant. He agreed. The problem is that I love being married. After my 1st divorce, I tried to remarry. I had several roughly 1-year-long romantic “investments” that flopped the moment I proposed marriage. I was spending so much time, energy, and resources, but all in vain.

Anatoli told me that I was doing it all wrong. Plus, I had a fundamental misconception. The right thinking and approach was: “All women are beautiful, and I should ask them to go to bed on the 2nd date. If they say no, just dump them.” I objected because I have a rule: avoid sex before marriage. (I learned that rule the hard way as a teenager. I broke it and had severe long-term psychological trauma.)

After some contemplation, we devised a suitable modification for me: “I love all women. Once I like a woman, I propose marriage the 2nd time I see her, or very shortly thereafter. If she says no, I dump her.” I agreed and made a covenant with him. (Women with husbands were to be excluded. From my point of view, it is right to avoid sharing wives.)

I truthfully held to Anatoli's covenant. Over the course of the next several years, I made about 20 proposals. Having been dumped so many times was an invaluable and intriguing experience. I loved it.

Eventually, my dream came true, and I got married for the 2nd time. Anatoli was actually directly involved. He facilitated my 2nd marriage and guided my wife and me. The day after his death, my 2nd marriage broke, and my 2nd wife divorced me.

Breakups really do hurt, but so many rejections of my marriage proposals made me strong and optimistic. So I recovered from my 2nd divorce in just 2 weeks. Of course, 3 weeks after my 2nd divorce, I proposed again. And lo and behold, I am entangled now. She calls me “my husband,” and I call her “my wife.”

Dear Anatoli, I am grateful for you. Once we meet in heaven, I will tell you the rest of my story.
