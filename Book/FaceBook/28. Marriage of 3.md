### (28) Marriage of 3 10/14/2026 #### FaceBook ##### Book

{Gifted to Raya} [I am moving to Colombia and giving away my collection of paintings, each with its own story.]

For all practical purposes, I am married and Anatoli is dead. But there are a few intriguing stories from the now-distant past worth telling.

Anatoli was about 30 years older than me and was very kind to stand in for my father. I was in a total mess after my 1st divorce, but he believed that he could fix me easily. And once he secured a covenant with me, there was a clear path.

The covenant: “I love all women. Once I like a woman, I propose marriage the 2nd time I see her, or very shortly thereafter. If she says no, I dump her.”

So the path for him to marry me was simple: he finds a woman that I may like, introduces me, and shortly thereafter I make a proposal. He thought I would be accepted, and we would be all set to live happily thereafter.

He made his 1st attempt by bringing me to a Christmas party and introducing me to his friend. I liked her, got her phone number, and proposed in a day or 2. She said she would consider it, but she was traveling to another state for New Year's Eve, and any further conversations would have to wait.

It could have been simple if Anatoli had been patient. On New Year's Eve, I was staying at home watching “Yes Man” (2008) with Jim Carrey. The next day, I was to work at 5 a.m. In the middle of the movie, after all those “Yes! Yes! Yes! Yes! ...”, Jim Carrey’s character and the woman he was falling in love with were reaching a turning point in their relationship on the stage of the Hollywood Bowl when my laptop battery died. It is one of the most interesting moments in the movie. Through all those absurd yeses, Jim Carrey’s character rediscovers joy after divorce, and she has the best punch line:

“I know our music isn't that mainstream... I love doing it. It's like my photography. I know there isn't that much demand for photographs taken while running, but you know, who cares? The world's a playground. You know that when you're a kid but somewhere along the way, everyone forgets it.”

I stood up to go to bed, but Anatoli entered and told me to come with him to a New Year's celebration, where he would introduce me to another friend of his. Why would he do that? Anyway, of course I said Yes! I liked her--love from the 1st hug--and proposed in a day or 2. She said she would consider it.

So I started that year with 2 marriage proposals under consideration. As an honest man who was once told to think like a woman when dealing with women, I decided that my 2 women-candidates would like an open dialogue. So I made a group chat, described the situation, and gave them options, including a marriage of the 3 of us. They turned out to know each other. They had a private call, etc., etc., etc.

Who cares how this story ended? Anatoli was fixing me very well.:) Thanks to Anatoli, I was cheering up after my 1st divorce, and I also got 2 similar but very different paintings to remember this happy, interesting, and intriguing incident.
