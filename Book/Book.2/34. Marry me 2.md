### (34) Marry me 2 10/20/2026 #### Book.2 ##### Book

Most romantic films are based on the fictional belief that people change easily. In contrast, change requires time, effort, determination, self-control, etc.--it is a long, hard process with a low success rate. Thus, it is most logical to find a person you like as is and propose marriage right away. Consider the movie "Marry Me" (2022) with Jennifer Lopez. At her concert, she proposes marriage to an acceptable stranger. He has always considered her adorable and agrees. The rest of the film is filled with drama and comedy as they try to figure out if their relationship will work out. (It did.)

This approach requires a lot of courage. One needs to recognize an opportunity, act on it, and commit if there is a positive response. In real life, this approach might work only for a few, but it is perfect for romantic comedies. If I were to write an opening for a mystical romantic comedy "Marry Me 2"...

---

**Opening:** He comes to a casino to play poker. There is a flirtatious, foxy lady at the table. She bluffs as a goddess. He feels some inexplicable, charming emanations coming from her. Can she be a witch?!? His mind weakens, and he loses time after time. Afterward, he compliments her and, as a joke, proposes to play another game--a simple bluff game called "Marry Me." She agrees.

**The game:** Three cards--the King, Queen, and Joker--are shuffled. One card is dealt to him, another to her, and one is set aside. Each player takes a peek at their own card. The goal is for the King and Queen to marry. The players engage in psychological questioning and attentive observation. If both players agree that they trust each other and are ready to marry, they flip over their cards. If either player wishes to decline, suspecting the other is the Joker, they flip over their own card and the one set aside. The game is considered won if the King and Queen are engaged.

**Plot:** Since he invented the game, he has an advantage when shuffling three cards. (If you know what you are up to, it is easy to arrive at a given setup when shuffling just three cards.) So, he deals her the Queen and himself the King. A simultaneously thoughtful and comical dialogue follows. He might elaborate on how he will treat her--as the King, he offers to allow her to do anything, provided that she does no harm. She might probe whether he meets her Queen's expectations, particularly whether he believes in magic. He says yes, and to prove his belief, he tells her that if they were to play the game again, he would be again the King, and she would be again the Queen. She agrees to test him, and they flip their cards, revealing his King-Queen setup. But she requests that, for the second round, she be the one to shuffle and deal the cards. He accepts with trepidation.

He is dealt the King. He wonders whether she is playing his own trick on him. The second round unfolds with the following short dialogue:  
-- [He] I am the King.  
-- [She] You need to say the magical words, and we'll flip our cards.  
-- [He] Will you marry me?  
-- [She] Yes.  
... [They flip their cards. She is the Queen.]  
-- [He] When would you like to schedule the wedding?  
-- [She] Next month, the same day.

He is perplexed. With all his heart, he has fallen in love, but he made his question sound like a joke. Yet she responded seriously, though she might have been bluffing. Unfortunately, he feels that if he asks her whether she cheated while dealing the cards, he will offend her.

The rest of the movie will be a witty comedy where the guy is perpetually frustrated in different situations because he cannot tell whether she is telling the truth or bluffing, whether she is setting him up in different situations, or whether she is actually a witch. Neither he nor the audience will know, but her behavior will plausibly support all those hypotheses. Anyway, he will love his sweet suffering of bewilderment.

**PS:** If you do not have cards handy but would still like to play this game with your girl, explain the rules, take three pieces of paper, and write "I love you" on each. She needs to be distracted, or you can write on the papers while holding them facing yourself, then place them face down. Allow her to shuffle and deal. Once she takes a peek, it is time to ask her, "Will you marry me?"
