You can easily find on the internet or ask ChatGPT about the original version of the story of Kisa Gotami. Following is my rewrite. It is a dark, chaotic comedy about embracing the religious diversity of Buddhism, Christianity, and Daoism and walking all the roads simultaneously.

Once upon a time, there lived Kisa. Supposedly, her infant died. Her feelings were very hurt. She objected: "NO, NO, and NO!!! My infant is NOT dead." She was walking around with the dead body of her infant, asking people for a cure. Everyone was turning her down until one day she met Buddha sitting under a tree.

Buddha was somewhat disturbed by the stressed mother and the decaying corpse of the infant. So instead of spending a lot of time enlightening Kisa, he simply said YES to Kisa, quickly taught her Vipassana meditation, and sent her to a nearby town to bring him a mustard seed from a house where "noone" had died.

She went away. Buddha buried the body of the infant. To avoid dealing with Kisa's complaints later, he sold his lot of land to Laozi and escaped to Nirvana.

Meanwhile, Kisa came to the nearby town. She was going from house to house, but everyone was telling her that either their parents, children, friends, pets, etc. had died. But luckily, she came to the house of Jesus. Jesus told her that in his house "noone" dies. Instead, "everyone" goes to heaven to live happily thereafter. Kisa rejoiced and asked Jesus to marry her in order to live in his fairytale happily thereafter, where "noone" dies. She even promised him to love thy neighbor, do what is right, embrace diversity, serve God, etc. Jesus said YES. But she took advantage of Jesus and requested that he reincarnate her infant. Jesus had options. Instead of making a miracle for Kisa, he made a choice to die for the sins of humanity and was crucified before the wedding.

So, Kisa became kind of a widow. She did a Vipassana meditation and went back to the tree where she had left the dead body of her infant with Buddha. But by that time, Laozi had built an orphanage there. He was taking care of cute little kids in orange robes whom he called monks.

Kisa asked Laozi to give back her infant. He said that her infant had grown and pointed to two orphans, saying, "He is your son, and she is your daughter." This koan was super tricky. If she says NO, he is NOT my son, and NO, she is NOT my daughter, then (1) she will insult the noble Laozi by accusing him of lying, (2) she will break the covenant with Jesus to love all and All, and (3) she will hurt the feelings of the children through rejection. However, if she replies YES to Laozi, (1) she will show respect to the noble Laozi, (2) she will stay true to her Christian faith, and (3) she will make her son and daughter happy.

After some Vipassana meditation, Kisa told Laozi, "May I have all my children back?" He lightened up and gladly gave her the keys to his orphanage. In return, she asked him, "Are you my husband?" She thought she gave Laozi a tricky koan. But Laozi simply liked her the moment she knocked on his door and obviously replied without any thinking, "YES, I am, but please promise that once we retire, we will do absolutely Nothing." She agreed and said YES again to doing absolutely Nothing later, when their children grew up.

Teamwork saves the day! Kisa learned how to say YES instead of NO.:) And Laozi got married >:)
