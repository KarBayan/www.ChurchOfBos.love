### (27) Yes Step at a Time 10/8/2026 #### Book.2 ##### Book

I love being in a positive mood. So I developed several approaches, one of which is to live one's life like an improv. The rule of improv is to say "yes, and..." (avoid saying "no" because it stops the action flow). Moreover, with the belief in God and His love, I realized that there is always a right option for me to say "yes" to. "And..." it's just a matter of me being willing to do what is right.

So on my flight to Colombia in August, the nice guy next to me said "Hi," and I replied, "Yes, and what is your name?" It turned out that he would be running for mayor in Maní. Moreover, he was interested in talking more and invited me to his town. I said "yes, and..."

"And..." shortly thereafter, once in Barbosa, I wrote him a message:

*Hi, Robin. Please understand that I am new to Colombia and some of my ideas might be in contradiction with local customs.*

*From my conversations with locals, I got the impression that mayor positions tend to be highly corrupt. Common people believe that the government provides enough money for municipalities to offer people decent services such as schooling, infrastructure, healthcare, roads, and other basic services. Common people believe that this money is usually stolen, though, from my point of view, there might be insufficient funds provided by the government in the first place or a lack of management experience.*

*I’m suggesting framing your intention to become mayor within a broader scope, thus assuring people that you will do the right thing. Specifically, I’m proposing to announce that you intend to form a mayors’ party. Former mayors with a proven track record are probably the best to represent local interests in Congress. Obviously, the mayors’ party will eventually have its presidential candidate. Do you wish to be Colombian president someday?:)*

*In addition to extra publicity, the announcement of the mayors’ party could attract the support of former mayors, respected municipal council members, and those who might be interested or are currently campaigning for a seat in Congress. Plus, you might be able to find mayoral candidates in other municipalities who would benefit from cooperation and campaigning.*

*Is this idea of any interest to you? If yes, we can discuss it further, make a plan, and proceed. We can try to solicit Mikhail Krasnov from Tunja or any other decent mayor or ex-mayor for consultations. I also met a young lawyer in Barbosa who is open to the idea of becoming a mayor. Maybe there can be other candidates to run for mayor next year under the umbrella of the mayors’ party (or mayors’ movement, if they want to be less ambitious).*

*What are your thoughts and feelings about this idea? Would you like to have a call to talk more?*

I heard "yes" in response, "and..." it was my turn to keep the momentum going. By that time, I had moved to Tunja. The right thing to do was to find the ex-mayor of Tunja, Mikhail Krasnov, the guy who, like me, was born in the USSR. ChatGPT failed to locate him, but the owner of Hotel San Francisco, where I was staying, asked me, "What would you like to do in Tunja?" I replied, "Yes, I want to find Mikhail Krasnov." "And..." he said that it was easy: "Mikhail lives in that high-rise building and teaches at the university, UPTC."

By the way, a very nice hotel in the center of town. The owner is also a good artist, and I really liked his painting of the moonwalk from the Apollo 11 mission: “That’s one small step for [a] man, one giant leap for mankind.”

I could have staked out Mikhail’s house, but I decided 1st to try finding him at UPTC. Moreover, because of my other "yes, and..." I was also planning to visit the Meteorology Department at UPTC. (That story requires its own chapter.)

Of course, I was stopped by the guard at the entrance of UPTC. While I was talking Russian-to-Spanish with the guard through ChatGPT, two students from the Physics Department stopped by out of curiosity. We conversed in English. They learned that I have a PhD in Physics, was looking to get to the Meteorology Department, was interested in politics, and was looking for Mikhail.

My problems were solved. They said "yes, and..." and informed the guard that I was going to a science conference and should be let in. As we were walking through campus, they pointed to the guy in a red T-shirt, who was Mikhail Krasnov. Of course, Krasnov (Краснов), whose last name literally translates from Russian as "The Red One," would be wearing a red T-shirt.:) "Yes, and..." we had lunch together. Mikhail suggested that we talk more.

Now I am back in Colombia. "Yes, and..." I am procrastinating on making a proposal to Mikhail about the next steps/ideas for building the mayors' party of Colombia. But thank God I met the most charming yes-lady. She said "yes" to my marriage proposal, "and..." she is going to visit me in Tunja. I am such a lucky yes-man to have my "yes, and..."-muse. I can do it. Maybe I will write the proposal tomorrow. >:)
