### (31) Camino 10/18/2026 #### Book.2 ##### Book

{Gifted to TBD} [I am moving to Colombia and giving away my collection of paintings, each with its own story.]

I was concerned when my friend suggested we walk the Camino Francés, about 500 miles (800 km)--a traditional pilgrimage from the French side of the border across Spain to Santiago, followed by a continuation to the Atlantic Ocean. Or maybe we are still young enough to do it slowly, in about 2 months?

She knew Spanish, and it was her dream to walk the Camino. My work was ending in August, and it would be a great opportunity for me to do some “spiritual activity.” The Camino can be a good travel opportunity, but if I combine it with reading the New Testament, I will turn it into a pilgrimage. Yes! I had always wished to read the New Testament from cover to cover, but I had never set aside enough time.

We started in the 1st week of September from Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port in France. It was hard at first to get used to walking for long hours, but we were steadily making progress. The problems started in October when the weather changed. It became cold and rainy. I’m a skinny guy, and I lose heat very quickly, especially when walking in pouring rain in a poncho. Combine this with carrying a backpack, and I’m losing energy faster than I can recover through rest and sleep. To make matters worse, I’ve been bitten by bedbugs, I have a rash, and I’m taking an antihistamine. But we made it to Santiago. In a few more days, we will be at Finisterre on the Atlantic Ocean.

We are leaving Santiago. The morning seems promising. It is sunny and really warm. I’m walking in a T-shirt. The cold wind and clouds arrive. I put on a sweater, a jacket, and a poncho, and it starts to rain. Half an hour later, the clouds are gone, the sun is back, the heat is intense, and I strip back down to my T-shirt. In another 30 minutes, the cold wind and rain return. And those 30-minute cycles of warming and cooling continue on and on. I’m exhausted. I’m getting a strange feeling in my chest. We stop for lunch. The waiter brings the chicken soup. My heartbeat becomes irregular. Everything starts to go dark. My friend inquires, “Are you going to throw up?” No, I’m going to have a heart attack. I am not scared; I am slowly contemplating the positive thought that it is probably better to have a heart attack than throw up in the restaurant. It is absurd and funny. I start laughing, and my heart rhythm stabilizes. I’m okay. (I kept my medical conditions to myself to avoid worrying my friend.)

We take a taxi to the albergue. We are sitting outside waiting to check in. The countryside is picturesque. It is sunny, but the cold wind picks up, and it starts to rain from a clear sky. There is such a beautiful rainbow, but I am going to have another heart attack. Next to us are a small cemetery and a church. The church bell starts to ring, and I start to laugh. The setting is surreal, and the timing is perfect. It would be the ideal, most poetic moment to die, if only I could be serious for a moment and stop laughing. Oh well, I’m okay again.

I truly enjoyed the Camino. I (1) helped my friend fulfil her dream, (2) read the New Testament, and (3) had the best laughs of my life.:)
