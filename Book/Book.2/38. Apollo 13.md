### (38) Apollo 13 10/30/2026 #### Book.2 ##### Book

Once upon a time, I came up with a plot similar to the messianic ‘Matrix’ (1999) by Wachowski. The corresponding film will lack fighting and special effects. It can be done on a low budget, akin to a proposal for Terminator 3 by Mad-TV (Terminator Vs. Jesus: The Greatest Action Story Ever Told, 1996). And, of course, love will be the savior.

**The background:** There is a secret international organization that deals with prophets, messiahs, and asuras. A long time ago, it became clear that there are special ones who can do anything and change the future.

Consider Mark 11:22:

> Then Jesus said to the disciples, “Have faith in God. I tell you the truth, you can say to the mountain, ‘May you be lifted up and thrown into the sea,’ and it will happen. But you must really believe it will happen and have no doubt in your heart. I tell you, you can pray for anything, and if you believe that you've received it, it will be yours. But when you are praying, first forgive anyone you are holding a grudge against, so that your Father in heaven will forgive your sins, too.”

Sometimes the special ones lack comprehension of their powers. For example, centuries ago, the weather forecast was free from the great flood, but Noah dreamed it in. [“If you can dream it, you can do it.”--Walt Disney] Would it have been less dramatic if Noah, instead of building an ark, had built a zoo to save rare animals from extinction? And would it have been better if Noah had addressed his fears of global warming and rising seas through therapy and reforestation activism? Those are precisely the situations the secret service organization is trying to avert.:)

**The plot:** There is a comet that appeared out of nowhere and is approaching Earth with a 100% guaranteed collision. This discovery was made 40 years ago, and now, finally, the prophet responsible for this event has been found. He is 44 years old, and the cause of this event is just one childish nightmare he cannot resolve.

In his own words: “Today I woke up and remembered that once, when I was a child, I had a [night] dream of making everyone happy. And if my mother had come that morning and kissed me, she would have woken me up, and I would have been the happiest child in the world. But this did not happen and will not happen because she died. Nevertheless, I still believe in miracles.”

This naïve worry about everyone being simultaneously happy led to a nightmare of a comet destroying Earth. As a child, he thought: if happiness for all is unattainable and everyone dies, why not all die simultaneously? By wishing so, everyone's misery would end. Now, as a grown-up, it would be the right thing for him to revisit his naïve childish belief that universal death is a compassionate solution. But he has simply never reexamined the emotional conviction beneath his beliefs. But, oh well, he just believes that the comet is coming, without even any actual factual knowledge that it will certainly arrive...

The guy is known to be hard to reason with, and obviously the comet will continue on its totally deadly trajectory even if he is killed. [There is already a plan B to go to the Moon, aka Project Apollo, which is in its late stages.] So, the secret service decides to use their special Chinese agent. She poses as a professor of computer science and befriends the guy. She is very bright and charming. Her goal is to convince him that much misinformation, especially on social media, is harmful to children. For example, some children believe that Earth is flat, that a comet is coming to destroy Earth, etc. She suggests he help her fight misinformation for the sake of the children. Her secret strategy is to persuade him that the comet is purely imaginary so that he changes his belief and erases the comet.

He kindly declines her proposal, effectively negating her strategy. Deception usually fails. >:) But he feels compassion for her stress and her lack of time for her kids, physical activities, hobbies, travel, etc. [She is divorced.] So, he decides to help her--she is also 44 years old and will probably live long enough to die when the comet arrives. He wishes her to be happy and will help her overcome everyday suffering in the remaining years before the total doom.

She fails her mission, but she finds warm feelings growing in her own heart in response to his kind offer of care. Anyway, there is disappointment in the secret service. She is to continue her mission until a new plan is devised. And one day, as usual, they are driving to the gym, and she receives a text that the comet has disappeared. She looks up from her screen, their eyes meet, and he tells her: “You know, I think... No. I feel... Anyway, I am sure I love you. Will you marry me? I promise that the ending will be like a fairy tale, like ‘they lived happily thereafter.’” She wonders: “Why?” And he replies: “I don't know. Would November 13th be a good day for the wedding?” He turns on the car radio, and ‘Mama This One's for You’ by Beth Hart starts and continues playing into the closing credits.
