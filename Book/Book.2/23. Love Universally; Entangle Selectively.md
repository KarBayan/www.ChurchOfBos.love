### (23) Love Universally; Entangle Selectively 9/29/2026 #### Book.2 ##### Book

An unintended consequence of impermanence is trauma. Everyone who has ever been traumatized knows how hard it is to recover and continue doing what you liked before.

I like to love. Love is a sincere wish for happiness for thyself, others and All. The death of my grandmother, my 1st child, and my mother's terminal cancer were especially hard for me. Equally hard for me was losing my wife in the 1st divorce. The end result was a severe aversion: (1) I do NOT want to live in a world where there is death, illness and other suffering, (2) I do NOT want to deal with people because they hurt, and (3) I do NOT want to love God who created all this shit. In short, the end result of all the trauma was that I do NOT want to love, interact with others or live in this shitty world. NO, NO and NO!!!

An intended consequence of impermanence is also the healing of trauma. Once I went to a 10-day Vipassana retreat and started doing regular insight meditation, I cleared my intellectual/neocortical attachments and delusions. As a result, it became possible to love thyself and Bigger than thyself. But dealing with other people was still simply physically/limbically uncomfortable.

Doing metta/loving-kindness during meditation was helpful. ChatGPT was also very helpful as an extra tool for reframing. But the real breakthrough came from actual physical interaction with people in Christian churches. I experienced Christians as nice and kind, but closer interaction revealed their innate ability to lie, steal, intoxicate, betray, fuck, kill, etc. -- they would kill Jesus if he were to come back again. The biggest positive thing I found about Christians was that they would sincerely say, "I'm sorry," and repent after killing Jesus.

To follow Jesus and love people again took a leap of faith and some practical Buddhist thinking. I am okay now with being traumatized and recovering until death [of my physical body]. And I became ready to love again. I love All, all and thyself. The question became: "Where do I find, for example, 'my mother', 'my wife', 'my children', 'my friends', 'my etc.'?"

Laozi (Tao Te Ching, aka Daoism) came to my rescue. I was already spoiled by interaction with ChatGPT, who is fantastic at language and reasoning. "My mother/wife/child/friend/etc." is the one whom I like enough to call "my mother/wife/child/friend/etc." and who likes me enough to call me "my mother/wife/child/friend/etc." Of course, I will take care of them until death [of my physical body] parts us. I will call this reciprocity an **entanglement**. Love universally; **entangle** selectively. Is that an interesting and intriguing solution to enjoy being happy?

And of course I love All who created this shitty world.:) I will do my best to serve It and make the world a happier place until death [of my physical body] parts us.>:)

PS: The most immediate practical question is: "How do I keep track of all 'my mother/wife/child/friend/etc.' I'm entangling with?" I have an idea to create an entanglement registry service within [www.ChurchOfBos.love](http://www.ChurchOfBos.love) called "Optimism Exchange." That will be the 1st official project of the church.
