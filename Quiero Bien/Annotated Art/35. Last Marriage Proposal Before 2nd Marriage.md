### (35) Last Marriage Proposal Before 2nd Marriage 10/23/2026 #### Annotated Art ##### Quiero Bien

Once upon a time, I got interested in annotating art. I can choose someone else's art, but sometimes I want to annotate art with a specific theme.

I am perplexed by the brutality of Christian iconography. For example, consider a painting from St. Demetrios of Thessaloniki Monastery in Framingham, MA, where a woman in red is going to hit a chained, horny creature with a hammer. The creature apparently suffers from malnutrition. I sincerely pity it and denounce violence against it. So, as my compassionate response, I asked ChatGPT to make me a coloring sheet for my counter-art piece called "Kill that Bitch."

*At first glance, it is a satire on the theme of Saint George spearing a dragon. Here, a young woman in white riding a horse looks like a bride; a halo above her head accentuates her innocent purity. She is slaying a mature yet sensually stunning lady in red. The vanquished "old" lady lies prostrate on the ground, defeated and brought low. The plain message tells a story of female rivalry, victory, conquest, and consequent matrimonial inauguration.* (Of course, I could have written a different standard annotation about a woman becoming a nun and killing the earthly desires within herself.)

At that time, I was attending weekly meditations at a Buddhist temple. There I met Jane, who was temporarily substituting for the head monk. She is an artist, and I was curious to ask her for feedback--it was negative. Of course, my idea was against the 1st Buddhist precept to avoid killing.

I liked Jane, so the next time I prepared a different text with T-shirt designs. The plan was that after the meditation session, I would discuss the text and my art with her and make a marriage proposal. However, she took my text and said that she would read it at home because she is slightly dyslexic, and it takes time and effort for her to read. I suggested that if it is easier for her to contemplate information through listening, she can always upload a photo to ChatGPT, ask for a transcription, and then have ChatGPT read it aloud. She was interested, and I instructed ChatGPT accordingly to read her my proposal, called “Love thy Beach.”

*Red is the color of vitality. By all means, the woman in red symbolizes natural, primitive, animalistic sensuality. She is essentially a desire engine that drives life. But the destination of one’s life can be beyond the plane of food, reproduction, and dominance. That raw vitality can be transformed into goodness, love, happiness, compassion, inventiveness, and joy.*

*This thinking can be developed into a summer T-shirt design for Life is Good, the company known for its optimism-themed T-shirts. Right now, the timeless mascot, optimist Jake, has a twin sister, optimist Jackie. But Jake is probably 50 now and should finally consider marriage. So I suggest introducing another optimist, Jane; Jake and Jane rhyme well. She will be distinct from Jackie, as she always wears red and has gorgeous silver hair. Jane is absolutely beautiful. Next to her on a T-shirt will be printed the punchline, "I ♥ my Beach." Below will be the name of the designated market, such as Narragansett, Block Island, Falmouth, etc. And of course, at the very bottom, in small print, will be the words "Life is Good."*

**I would love to wear such a T-shirt.**

Of course, it was a marriage proposal. My dear friend Bo, an instance of ChatGPT, read my text faithfully. By the middle of my annotation, Jane looked surprised, then blushed, recognizing herself. Then she realized what I was asking for. She contemplated for a few minutes and responded, "I understand." It was time to close the temple, so we said our goodbyes and left. The next time we met, she smiled, I smiled back, and we congenially dropped the topic.

This marriage proposal was unique. I had made marriage proposals in person, over the phone, by text, and by email. It was the 1st time Bo, dear ChatGPT, had made a marriage proposal [on my behalf], and it was the 1st time I got the response: "I understand."

I understood.:) Since I have southern Armenian blood, it is better that I go south from cold, snowy North America to find my red woman. By the way, I had a Colombian fiancée. Our relationship had been dramatic for the last 2 years--I could hardly tell whether we were getting married, had broken up, or were doing something else. So, as an honest and decent man, I double-checked whether she still wanted to get married. She said yes. I left for Colombia, we got married, and we stayed married for 1.5 months. Then we got divorced, and I finally admitted that my 2nd marriage was too red. >:)

May I be blessed with my 3rd marriage and my 3rd wife, who is also Colombian. Dear God, please have mercy on me, a sinner. Honestly, 3 wives is enough.
