### (6) Financial Challenges 9/10/2026

It is hard to say if I am an opportunistic optimist, or if my decision to found a church and serve God paid off, or both, or something else. But I wished for a happy, interesting, and intriguing (mysterious) life, and I am loving what I am getting.

The most intriguing challenge of my third marriage was a financial one. Without work in Colombia, I thought that I would be enjoying an early retirement and living off my savings. But shortly after I got married and arrived in rural Colombia, in Barbosa, I got locked out of my finances. I was making ATM withdrawals, but after a few weeks my bank card stopped working. To my great frustration, Bank of America's automated service firmly declined to connect me to a customer service representative until it could help me solve my problem. After about 30 minutes of back and forth, I plainly told it, "You suck." It replied, "Sorry, goodbye," and hung up the phone. It was too late to apologize for hurting its feelings.

Just in case, I had another card from Digital Credit Union, but I forgot the PIN. So I decided to open an account with Bancolombia and do a transfer using Remitly. But the clerk in my little town declined my application until I brought a RUT, a paper showing that I am ready for taxable economic activity. Her manager concurred and told me to hire an accountant.

Using my last cash on hand, I hired an accountant. The hardworking guy and his wife tried to obtain a RUT online. Failed. They had a video appointment. Failed. They sent me on a 2-hour drive to Tunja. Failed. (Tunja has about 190,000 people. That is about 6 times bigger than Barbosa.)

Out of desperation, I stopped at the main branch of Bancolombia in Tunja, and to my great surprise, they proceeded to open me a bank account without a RUT. But they had a glitch in the system, issued a support ticket, and asked me to come again the next day. It took another 2-hour drive the next day and 2 more support tickets before my high hopes were crushed. It turned out that someone else in their system had opened an account with an ID document having the same number as my U.S. passport. Since the ID number is the key field in their database, the collision was fatal for my expectations; they told me to give up and maybe wait for their call next week. They never called.

The little money that I had, I spent feeding carrots to my favorite red-loving calf. Not to mention that my wife divorced me and asked me to leave the house. So, with the bags packed, I went to my accountant and asked him to return my payment because our contract was that they would get me a RUT. My accountant counter-offered to go along with me to the local Bancolombia branch in Barbosa. I agreed.

At the bank, the same clerk greeted us with the words, "I am not going to open him an account without RUT." My accountant countered that I already had a record in their system, and I took out my Massachusetts driver's license and told her to use my secondary ID to complete the process. Lo and behold, I got a bank account and a fancy yellow card.

Massachusetts driver's licenses are seemingly more valuable in Colombia than my U.S. passport. They look almost identical to Colombia's national ID card. Of course, even Colombians who read only Spanish eagerly assume my Massachusetts driver's license is a valid U.S. national ID.

Due to the divorce, I moved from Barbosa to Tunja, where I got a tentative offer to teach English and Russian. So I asked my accountant to make an appointment for a RUT in Bogotá. (Bogotá has about 8 million people and is about a 3-hour drive from Tunja.) The only available spot was September 5th.

In the afternoon, I made a marriage proposal. Later in the evening, the moment I completed my appointment and finally obtained a RUT, the document that will allow me to teach in Colombia, I got a text confirming acceptance of my marriage proposal. The next day at 4 a.m., I flew back to the U.S.

Note that I got the Colombian work authorization, RUT, on Saturday of the U.S. Labor Day weekend. Only after I got the RUT did I get the marriage acceptance. The obvious interpretation of those signs is that my new wife wants me to work. In return, I want her to learn how to meditate.:)
