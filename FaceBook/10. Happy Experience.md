### (10) Happy Experience 9/16/2026

{Gifted to Ed} [I am moving to Colombia and giving away my collection of paintings, each with its own story.]

The story begins when I took my car to a mechanic to drop it off for repair. Behind the counter was a stunning painting of 2 wolves. I asked him who had done it. He replied that he had accepted it as payment for his work from one of his clients. The painting radiated such intense emotional expression and talent that I offered to cover the expenses he had incurred. He said that it would be about $1K, but he would not sell the painting; instead, he gave me the person's phone number.

I called the number. It turned out to be a young Ukrainian lady. She had escaped the war and had already been in the U.S. for a year, learning English and trying to live on some occasional part-time jobs. She had a degree in landscape design but no formal education in painting. From time to time, "feelings" would build up in her, and she would paint for 6 to 8 hours straight before "relieving" herself. She would complete a painting as a marathon and then be exhausted for several weeks. For her, it would be a moment of reaching for what is "bigger than thyself." And she could not create comparably, for example, by working less intensely but over a longer period of time. This explosive, performance-type artistry is valid, similar to the all-consuming performance of athletes at a final or singers during a concert.

I believed that she had talent and could be successful in the long run, as she could perform reliably on about a monthly basis without drugs, alcohol, or any other stimulants. So, to support her and motivate her to continue drawing, I bought those 4 paintings for $1K each and commissioned her to draw me another one for $1K. (This type of art is priceless—even a car mechanic cannot part with it for $1K.:)

Unfortunately, she decided to move to California. Though I was still recovering from the divorce, I proposed marriage to her—we had been friends for some time by then. She declined, and we settled for a quick pencil portrait of me.
