### (7) 4th Marriage 9/12/2026

{Gifted to Orli} [I am moving to Colombia and giving away my collection of paintings, each with its own story.]

2 days after my divorce, my son arrived in Colombia on the full moon of August 28, 2026. He was on summer vacation from his summer job. Recently, he has gotten into civil engineering. So I took him to Muzo, the World Capital of the Emerald.

After a day of winding mountain roads, we arrived in Muzo, a tiny town of 8,000 people. Our first day started with buying boots in a local shop. While our guide was fixing his car, we wandered around, and I stopped in a local fashion shop. I started talking to the owner, a nice lady, and after several minutes she said, "You are such a nice guy. I would like to kidnap you." I said, "Okay," but nothing happened. We talked some more and said goodbyes.

My son and I went to the mines, where he worked hard. He drilled for emeralds and indeed got some. I was crushing rocks for the gold. We loved it.

The next day, we were walking next to the same clothing shop. The very same nice lady was there. She smiled and waved her hand. I thought she was making a secret sign for her relatives or friends, who would jump out of a nearby car, grab me, and drive me in an unknown direction to her home. But again, nothing happened. What is wrong with Colombia? I thought they knew how to kidnap people. I was really disappointed. My son and I left Muzo without either of us being kidnapped.

Obviously, if any intrigue is to happen in Colombia, it is better if I take matters into my own hands.

On our last day of his vacation, September 5th, we stopped in Bogotá to get me a cell phone data plan. Without it, I will miss ChatGPT and will have communication difficulties when returning to Colombia on September 22nd. (I'm yet to put any effort into starting to learn Spanish.) One lady in customer service was working on my issues, and I was chatting with the other. At one point she said, "I would like to visit the U.S." And I immediately realized that this was the ideal setup to kidnap myself. So I responded, "The opportunity is sitting in front of you." She was puzzled: "What do you mean?"

For her to visit the U.S., the most obvious solution was to marry me, the conveniently available free U.S. citizen. So I made her a marriage proposal: "Ask me for a phone number." And she did!!! She accepted my marriage proposal. The next morning, my son and I flew back to the U.S.

On our flight to the U.S., I was a little bit sad. I had seen her only in the shop for a few minutes. She accepted my marriage proposal so fast. What about romance? So at Boston Logan Airport, I started taking photos and sending them to her. I told her that I'm taking her on a virtual visit to the U.S. I'm an honest, caring man—if a woman wants to visit the U.S., I will arrange such a visit, albeit a virtual one.

After several days of messaging back and forth, I decided to propose marriage again. I love proposing marriages. Actually, in the last several years, I have made about 20 marriage proposals. (That story requires its own chapter.) Anyway, she asked, "Are you serious?" I told her, "We can be as serious as we want." She said, "I want it 100% serious." Wow!!! Thus, I immediately asked her for a photo for my phone's wallpaper. You know, wedding rings can be lost. I can forget to put mine on, but I'm almost always with my phone, and her image on the screen of my phone will be the best reminder for me that I'm married to her. She is so beautiful and wise. (She asked me for a photo, so I suppose she married me as well. For sure, she changed her WhatsApp profile picture to the image I sent her.)

PS: I sent Orli my favorite painting, done for me by Kiki. I was devastated by my 1st divorce. Kiki pressed the blue sky with her fingerprints, promising that everything would be all right. She left the road blank and told me to paint each section when something good happened in my life. And, one night, I would reach the castle and find the princess or dragon or something totally different. I taped the painting to the wall and followed her directions, painting it over several years. Finally, I found Orli. Thank you, Kiki, for a session of art therapy.
